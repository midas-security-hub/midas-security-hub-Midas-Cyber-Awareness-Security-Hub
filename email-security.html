Attackers hide in plain sight. Hover over or tap any red numbered hotspot (1 to 7) to reveal warning signs.

🚩 7 Red Flags in This Email

1
Suspicious sender address
The email is not from an official Microsoft domain. Check for misspellings or unfamiliar domains.
💡 TIP
Real Microsoft emails come from @microsoft.com, not look-alike domains.
2
Unexpected recipient
You weren't expecting this email or it was sent to a generic address / mass recipient list.
💡 TIP
Attackers often send phishing emails to many people at once.
3
Urgent timing
Early morning or outside normal business hours. Creates pressure to act fast.
💡 TIP
Urgency is a common trick to make you rush and ignore the warning signs.
4
High-pressure subject line
Uses panic phrases like URGENT: Action Required to force compliance.
💡 TIP
Legitimate IT teams give advance notice and don't threaten lockouts.
5
Deceptive call-to-action link
Button points to a fake domain (microsoft-security.com) to steal credentials.
💡 TIP
Always hover over links before clicking to inspect the true URL destination.
6
Dangerous attachment (.html / .exe)
Contains Account_Verification_Form.html which executes malicious scripts.
💡 TIP
HTML attachments can open fake login forms offline or execute scripts.
7
Report Phishing button in Outlook
The built-in Outlook Report button allows 1-click incident escalation to SOC.
💡 TIP
Reporting phishing alerts SOC immediately and protects all company colleagues.
How to Report in Outlook
1

Open the email

Select the suspicious email in your inbox list.

2

Click Report

Click the Report button in the top Outlook toolbar.

🛡️ Report ▾
3

Choose Report Phishing

Select Report Phishing from the dropdown menu.

4

You're all set!

The email & attachment will be sent securely to the Midas SOC team.
