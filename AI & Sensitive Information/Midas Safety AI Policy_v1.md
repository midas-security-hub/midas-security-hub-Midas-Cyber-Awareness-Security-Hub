## **Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy** 

_Responsible, Secure, and Effective Use of AI Across MIDAS SAFETY_ 

Version 1.0  |  Effective Date: [Aug 24, 2026]  |  Supersedes: Nil | Owner: AI Steering Committee  |  Next Review: Aug 2027 

### **1. Purpose** 

MIDAS SAFETY embraces Artificial Intelligence (AI) as a strategic enabler to improve productivity, innovation, decisionmaking, customer service, and operational excellence. 

This policy establishes the principles, rules, and procedures governing the responsible use of AI across Midas Safety, ensuring appropriate protection of company information, customer data, intellectual property, and employee privacy, while enabling employees to adopt AI with confidence and clarity. 

### **2. Scope** 

This policy applies to: 

1. All employees, contractors, consultants, and third parties conducting work on behalf of MIDAS SAFETY. 

2. Any AI tool used for company business, whether accessed directly, via browser extension, or through mobile applications. 

3. AI capabilities embedded within approved business applications (e.g. AI features inside CRM, HR, or productivity suites). 

4. Generative AI, machine learning, predictive analytics, automation/RPA, and future AI technologies utilized by MIDAS SAFETY. 

5. AI tools used on personal devices when processing company information ("Bring Your Own AI"). 

### **3. Definitions** 

1. **Approved AI Platform:** An AI tool or platform formally reviewed and authorized by IT/Information Security and listed in the Approved AI Tools Register (Appendix A). 

2. **Public AI Tool:** Any consumer-facing AI service not on the Approved AI Tools Register, including free or personalaccount versions of otherwise-approved products. 

3. **Confidential Information:** Company, customer, supplier, or employee data not intended for public disclosure, including the classifications defined in Section 6. 

4. **AI-Generated Output:** Any content, code, decision, prediction, or recommendation produced wholly or partly by an AI system. 

5. **Human-in-the-Loop:** A requirement that a qualified employee reviews and validates AI output before it is relied upon, acted on, or shared. 

### **4. Our AI Principles** 

1. **Human Accountability:** AI supports decision-making, but accountability always remains with people. Employees are responsible for reviewing and validating AI-generated outputs before use. 

2. **Responsible and Ethical Use:** AI shall be used in a manner consistent with MIDAS SAFETY's values, code of conduct, and applicable laws. AI must never be used for unlawful, deceptive, discriminatory, or unethical activities. 

3. **Transparency:** Users should disclose AI assistance where required (internally or to clients/regulators) and ensure AI-generated content is reviewed for accuracy before being relied upon or shared externally.

4. **Data Security:** Employees must protect confidential company, customer, supplier, and employee information when using AI systems. Confidential information may only be processed using approved company-authorized AI platforms, per the classification rules in Section 6. 

5. **Fairness and Non-Discrimination:** AI used in decisions affecting people — including hiring, performance, promotion, or customer treatment — must be checked for bias and must not be the sole basis for such decisions. 

6. **Continuous Learning:** The Company is committed to evolving its AI practices with technological and regulatory change, and to providing opportunities for employees to upskill. 

### **5. Application of AI — Approved Use** 

Approved AI tools and platforms (see Appendix A) may be used for: 

1. Drafting emails, reports, presentations, and policies. 

2. Data analysis and reporting. 

3. Meeting summaries and note-taking. 

4. Translation and language support. 

5. Research and productivity improvement. 

6. Software development and troubleshooting. 

7. Process documentation and knowledge management. 

#### **5.1 Requesting Access to a New AI Tool** 

1. Submit a request to IT with the tool name, intended use case, and data types involved. 

2. IT/Information Security perform a security and data-privacy review (including vendor data-retention and modeltraining terms). 

3. For tools intended to process Restricted data (Section 6) or used enterprise-wide, the request is escalated to the relevant C-Level Manager and, where material, the AI Steering Committee. 

4. Approved tools are added to the Approved AI Tools Register (Appendix A) with the permitted data classification and use cases.

- **6.1  Approval Process for Restricted Information** 

   1. Confirm the AI platform is on the Approved AI Tools Register and is authorized for Restricted data. 

   2. Obtain written approval from your manager or the relevant data owner (e.g. HR for employee data, Finance for unpublished financials, Legal for contracts) before proceeding. 

   3. Use only the minimum data necessary (data minimization) — anonymize or redact identifiers where the task allows. 

   4. Record the approval per departmental record-keeping practice in case of audit. 

### **7. Prohibited Use** 

Employees shall not: 

1. Enter company, customer, supplier, or employee information into unapproved public AI tools. 

2. Use AI to make final decisions without human review ("human-in-the-loop" is mandatory). 

3. Represent AI-generated output as verified fact without independent review. 

4. Allow AI systems to make financial, contractual, or personnel decisions autonomously. 

5. Use AI to generate content that is discriminatory, deceptive, defamatory, or infringes third-party intellectual property rights. 

6. Use AI to bypass internal controls, approval workflows, or segregation-of-duties requirements. 

7. Upload source code, credentials, encryption keys, or security configurations to any tool not explicitly approved for that purpose. 

### **8. Intellectual Property and AI-Generated Content** 

1. Employees must not input third-party copyrighted material into AI tools in a manner that infringes the rights holder's terms. 

2. AI-generated content created in the course of employment is company property, consistent with existing IP and employment agreements. 

3. Before publishing or filing AI-assisted work (e.g. patents, marketing copy, external reports), confirm with Legal whether disclosure of AI involvement or additional IP review is required. 

4. Do not represent AI-generated creative or technical work as solely human-authored where authorship materially affects legal protection or client expectations. 

### **9. AI in People-Related Decisions** 

Where AI tools are used to support recruitment, performance evaluation, promotion, disciplinary, or compensation processes: 

1. AI output may inform but never solely determine the outcome; a qualified human decision-maker must review and approve. 

2. HR and the AI Steering Committee must review and approve any such tool before deployment, including an assessment of bias and disparate-impact risk. 

3. Affected employees or candidates must be informed that AI was used in the process where required by applicable law.

### **10. Third-Party and Vendor AI** 

1. AI features embedded in vendor or SaaS applications (e.g. CRM, HRIS, finance systems) must be reviewed by IT/Information Security before activation, even if the underlying application is already approved. 

2. Procurement must confirm vendor data-handling, model-training, and data-residency terms as part of any new contract involving AI functionality. 

3. Vendors must not use Midas Safety's confidential data to train models available to other customers, unless explicitly contracted otherwise. 

### **11. Governance** 

An AI Steering Committee shall oversee AI adoption and governance across Midas Safety. The Committee will: 

1. Define AI strategy and priorities. 

2. Review and approve higher-risk AI initiatives, including tools processing Restricted data or supporting peoplerelated decisions. 

3. Maintain and publish the Approved AI Tools Register (Appendix A). 

4. Monitor compliance and risks, including periodic audits of AI tool usage. 

5. Promote AI adoption and capability building. 

6. Review this policy annually, or following material regulatory or technological changes. 

### **12. Training and Awareness** 

AI capability is a shared responsibility. The company will provide developmental opportunities, exposure, and support to help employees build AI literacy and skills, including mandatory onboarding training on this policy and periodic refreshers. 

Managers are expected to actively encourage appropriate AI adoption within their functions and identify opportunities to improve efficiency, innovation, and decision-making through AI. Employees are expected to take ownership of their continuous learning and proactively develop the competencies required for future success. 

### **13. Reporting, Compliance, and Enforcement** 

Any suspected misuse, data leakage, security concern, or inappropriate AI activity must be reported immediately to IT or Information Security for investigation and corrective action. Reports may be made confidentially and in good faith without fear of retaliation. 

Violations of this policy will be addressed under Company’s standard disciplinary process and may, depending on severity, result in restricted system access, formal disciplinary action up to and including termination, and, where applicable, legal action. Contractors and third parties in breach may have engagements suspended or terminated. 

### **14. Legal and Regulatory Alignment** 

This policy operates alongside, and does not replace, Midas Safety's obligations under applicable data protection, employment, consumer protection, and sector-specific regulations. Where local law imposes stricter requirements than this policy, local law prevails. Legal and Compliance should be consulted for cross-border data transfers arising from AI tool use. 

_This policy serves as the foundation for internal procedures, operational guidelines, and stakeholder communications related to AI. All employees and partners are expected to uphold these principles in their use of AI._
